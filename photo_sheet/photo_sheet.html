Passport Photo Sheet

Turns an ordinary photo into a passport picture: a file at the size an online form asks for, or a 4 x 6 inch sheet of six copies to print.

Nothing you type or choose leaves your device. Close the page and it is gone, except the size you last picked, which it remembers in this browser. Your photo never leaves this browser: nothing is uploaded.

  1. Press Try it with a drawn face to see how it works (it loads a drawn face and makes a finished picture straight away), or Choose photo for your own (how: a phone opens its camera or pictures; a PC asks for a JPEG or PNG file).
  2. Drag the picture in the crop box until the head sits inside the guide; pick the size the form asks for.
  3. Press Save one picture or Make print sheet. The result appears on the page under the button: press and hold it (phone) or right-click (PC) and choose Save. Nothing has to download.

2. Size

Sizes are in mm (millimetres) and px (pixels, the dots that make up a digital picture). The link between them is dpi [dots per inch: how many pixels fit in one printed inch; 300 is normal for photos].

What this can and cannot tell you

It can crop, brighten, resize and lay out your photo at the exact size you type, and tell you the file size it made. It cannot remove a background, check your face, or promise an office will accept the photo: the guides are general guidance, and the issuing body decides.

Where the numbers come from

The 2 x 2 in size, with the head 1 to 1 3/8 in tall and the eyes 1 1/8 to 1 3/8 in from the bottom, follows the US Department of State's published guidance. 35 x 45 mm is a common international size with no single head rule, so the page draws only a faint dashed oval as a rough aid (head about 70-80% of the photo height, which many such rules fall near); the issuing body decides. 300 dpi and the 4 x 6 in sheet are print-lab conventions; 25.4 mm = 1 inch exactly. All are marked "verify with the issuing body" in the preset table.
