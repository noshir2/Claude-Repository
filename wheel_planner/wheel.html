Options wheel

One page for the puts and covered calls you sell on Webull: how the trades you already made did, whether a new one is worth selling, whether a roll is worth it, what happens if you are assigned, and whether you can afford assignment this week.

Nothing you type or choose leaves your device. Close the page and it is gone, except the numbers you type in the boxes (never your trade file), which it remembers in this browser so you do not have to retype them.

3. Should I roll?

Rolling means buying back the put you sold and selling a new one with a later expiry. This tells you whether the swap pays you or costs you, and where your break-even moves.

The boxes below hold made-up example numbers, not yours. They are not remembered. Type over them, or press "Clear this section" to start with empty boxes.
  1. From your open position in Webull, note the put you have now: its strike, expiry, the premium you received, and the price to buy it back now (the ask).
  2. From the option chain, note the new put you would sell: strike, expiry, and the premium you would receive (the bid).

What this can and cannot tell you

  • Section 1 counts cash only: money from contracts that are finished (bought back, expired, or assigned), minus the cost of buying contracts back. Fees and taxes are not included. Shares you still hold are shown separately because their value is not settled.
  • Webull's file does not say when you were assigned. The page guesses from a matching share purchase or sale within four days of expiry; otherwise it counts the contract as expired and lists it under "Please check" so you can correct it.
  • Shares bought before your file starts, options sold short before expiry (early assignment) and shorting stock are not handled.
  • Sections 2 to 5 use only the numbers you type: no live prices. The "rough odds" come from a textbook formula (Black-Scholes) that ignores earnings jumps and dividends. It is the chance of ending past the strike at expiry, not the chance of touching it on the way, which is roughly double.
  • The rules in section 2 are your thresholds, written down when calm. The page repeats them as sentences and never gives a verdict on whether to trade.
  • Estimates from numbers you typed; no live data; the odds are a rough model; this is not investment advice.

Where the numbers come from

  • Webull holds the full strike x 100 as collateral for a cash-secured put (Webull help pages on assignment and the cash-secured put lesson).
  • Return on collateral = premium / collateral; annualized = that x 365 / calendar days to expiry: the convention of the common wheel calculators (TradesViz, QuantWheel, tastylive's return on capital).
  • Rough odds = N(-d2) for a put and N(d2) for a call from the Black-Scholes formula with no dividends; 1-sigma expected move = price x IV x sqrt(days / 365) (tastylive, Cboe, the Wikipedia article on Black-Scholes).
  • Roll math: net = new premium - buyback; new break-even = new strike - everything collected (tastylive and QuantWheel on rolling for a credit).
  • Covered-call break-even = what you paid - premium, and "a strike below what you paid locks in a loss" (Webull Learn on covered calls).
  • Leveraged funds reset daily and can lose value while the index ends flat (ProShares and Direxion fund documents). The built-in list goes stale; check the issuer.
  • Interest rate default 4%: the 4-week Treasury bill rate in October 2026. It changes daily.
