Fantasy Lineup Helper

Tells you who to start this week, whether a trade helps your starting lineup, and which free-agent pickup adds the most, using the projection files you download from FantasyPros and your own league's scoring.

Nothing you type or choose leaves your device. Close the page and it is gone, except your team lists, league names and scoring settings, which it remembers in this browser.

Example data is loaded: made-up players, made-up files, a made-up league. Nothing you change while the example is loaded is saved. Your own saved teams are untouched: press one of the two league buttons in step 2 (your own teams), or choose your own files in step 1, and the page brings your own team, scoring and files back.

1. Your projection files

How: on FantasyPros, open Projections, pick the week, open each position (QB, RB, WR, TE, K, DST) and press the download or export button; the names of buttons change from time to time. Save all six files where you can find them, then choose them all here at once (hold Ctrl on a PC, or pick them one at a time).

      2. Your team

      How: type or paste the names from your Yahoo roster page, one per line, name only (no injury letter, no opponent). Spelling does not have to be perfect; the page shows you what it matched. You can add a comma and the position after a name (QB, RB, WR, TE, K or DEF), which helps when two players share a name. Lines pasted straight from Yahoo like "Passer Baker Dal - QB" are fine: the page drops the team and position and shows you what it read. For a defense write the city or team name, for example "Chicago D/ST".
      Which league?

      This is the example team, and what you type here is not saved. Press one of the league buttons above to get your own team back.

      3. Your league's scoring and starting spots

      These start as Yahoo's default settings (half a point per catch). Check your league's settings page on Yahoo; defaults differ from league to league and a commissioner can change anything. The page remembers what you type here for each league.

      Starting spots

      How many players you start at each spot. Yahoo's default is 1 QB, 2 RB, 2 WR, 1 TE, 1 flex (RB/WR/TE), 1 K, 1 DEF.

      What the page says

      Compares your best starting lineup before and after the swap. The number that matters is the change in your starting lineup's total, not the players' raw points: a player who would sit on your bench adds nothing.

      Players you give (tick them)

      Ranks free agents by how much they would raise your best starting lineup, not by their raw points. A 15-point receiver who would sit behind your starters ranks below a 10-point kicker who would start.

      What this can and cannot tell you

      • The projections are FantasyPros' opinion as of your download. This page only ranks them under your league's scoring; it does not predict games.
      • It only knows what is in the files: no injuries, no bye weeks, no opponents. A player on bye is missing from the file, so he shows up under "not projected this week".
      • Some scoring cannot be worked out from the file and is ignored: 2-point conversions, return touchdowns, blocked kicks, field-goal distances, defensive yards allowed. Expect small differences from your league's final numbers.
      • Kickers: the file has no field-goal distances, so every field goal is worth one average number (3.3 estimates Yahoo's 3, 4 and 5). FantasyPros' own column uses a flat 3, so kickers show a small, expected "please check" note.
      • The floor and ceiling for a defense are shaky: FantasyPros' high and low rows for defenses do not always agree with the main row.
      • The file layout was built from FantasyPros' pages and from public copies of these files, 2019 to 2026. If FantasyPros changes its columns, the page says which headings it found and which it expected.
      • Football only. The trade check does not look at bye-week coverage after the trade.

      Where the numbers come from

      • Projected points are recomputed by this page from the stat columns in the file (yards, touchdowns, catches and so on) with the points in step 3. Floor uses the "low" row FantasyPros ships under each player (a bad game), ceiling the "high" row (a great game) or, when there is none, projection plus the gap down to the floor.
      • File columns: the FantasyPros projection pages (fantasypros.com/nfl/projections/qb.php and the other positions) and more than twenty public copies of exported files, 2019 to 2026.
      • What FantasyPros' own points column (called FPTS in the file) is made of: fantasypros.com/scoring-settings/, checked by arithmetic on exported rows. It follows whichever tab (Standard, Half PPR or PPR; PPR means a point per catch) was selected when you downloaded.
      • Yahoo's default starting spots and scoring: Yahoo's help page "Default league settings in Fantasy Football" (help.yahoo.com/kb/SLN6489.html) and the flex definition (help.yahoo.com/kb/SLN6500.html). That page is undated and a few years old; verify every number against your league's settings page for this season.
      • The best lineup is an exact search over every legal way to fill the spots, so the flex really is the best leftover RB, WR or TE. Ties go to the higher floor. The Python version of this tool in the same folder is the tested reference and gives the same numbers (advanced users only).
      • FantasyPros' Terms of Use allow one personal copy of their data. Download the files yourself and do not publish them. The example players and teams on this page are invented.
