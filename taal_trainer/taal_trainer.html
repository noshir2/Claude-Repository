Taal Trainer

A practice metronome for Indian classical music: it counts every beat of a taal [a rhythm cycle] out loud and on screen, so you can clap, wave and recite along with it.

Nothing you type or choose leaves your device. Close the page and it is gone, except the few settings it remembers in this browser (your last taal, the tempo, practice minutes per day and the game's best score).

  1. Pick a taal below. Teentaal, with 16 beats, is the usual first one and is already selected.
  2. Press the big green Start button. The beat sounds and the yellow highlight moves across the grid.
  3. Clap on X and on the numbered beats, wave on 0, and say the word shown. Press Stop when you are done.

Sound starts with that first press. The beat shows in the readout and grid below.

What the words mean

theka [the standard drum pattern of a taal]; bol [one spoken drum syllable, such as Dha]; matra [one beat]; vibhag [a group of beats, drawn as a box in the grid]; sam [the first beat of the cycle, shown as X]; taali [a clap beat, shown as 2, 3, 4]; khali [a wave beat with an empty sound, shown as 0].

Thekas vary by teacher and gharana [the school or family tradition a player learned in]; confirm with your teacher. Entries marked "confirm" are a common form; a teacher may use different bols or divisions. Edit the table at the top of this file.

What this can and cannot tell you

It can keep a steady cycle, show where the claps and waves fall, hide the words so you recite from memory, and count your practice minutes. It cannot judge your hands or voice, play a real tabla, or tell you which version of a theka your teacher or exam board expects: thekas vary, so confirm with your teacher. Every beat is a whole beat: a half-beat bol is shown as written, but the click lands on the beat.

Where the figures come from

The taal tables (beats, groups, clap and wave positions, bols) follow common textbook forms as given on Wikipedia's taal pages, chandrakantha.com and the University of Washington theka list; each taal carries its own source line in the table at the top of this file. The laya ranges are a teaching simplification, not a rule. Check anything that matters for an exam against your teacher and the exam body's current syllabus.
